# Credits page research and design

Reviewed November 22, 2026. The goal is a public record that recognizes actual work while leaving clear room for future student, mentor, IT, and school contributors. The examples below informed the structure; the resulting page and copy are original to Pioneer Clubs.

| Source | Observed approach | Applied to Pioneer Clubs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Mozilla contributors](https://www.mozilla.org/credits/) and [credits FAQ](https://www.mozilla.org/credits/faq/) | A durable public list with a contribution threshold and a route to request addition. | Keep a living record with a correction path; add names after the contribution is confirmed. |
| [Greenleaf Center for Servant Leadership](https://greenleaf.org/what-is-servant-leadership/) | Service begins with attention to the needs and growth of other people and the wider community. | Describe Aarti's work as the beginning of a shared resource for PCSS clubs. Avoid a self-awarded leadership title or superlative. |
| [Center for Creative Leadership](https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/what-is-leadership-a-definition/) | Leadership is a shared process involving direction, alignment, and commitment. | Show the project as something students, mentors, IT teams, and administrators can build together. |
| [Khan Academy leadership](https://www.khanacademy.org/about/leadership) | People are grouped by distinct organizational roles, including advisors and leadership. | Give club mentors and school administration their own visible areas, without naming unconfirmed people. |
| [Code.org leadership](https://code.org/en-US/about/leadership) | Leadership, education council, and long-term advisors receive separate recognition. | Separate guidance from building and technical support. The sections describe contributions, not institutional endorsements. |
| [GitHub on non-code contributions](https://github.com/readme/featured/open-source-non-code-contributions) and [maintainer recognition](https://github.com/readme/guides/onward-maintainer-framework) | Writing, community support, design, and maintenance count alongside code. | Credit student writing and stories, IT operations, and mentoring as real work rather than an afterthought. |
| [W3C heading guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/page-structure/headings/), [USWDS cards](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/card/), and [USWDS typography](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/typography/) | Headings make longer pages navigable; readable type and cards help readers scan grouped content. | Use one page title, named sections, and compact, readable person entries. Keep Aarti's name at the same visual scale as every future contributor. |
| [U.S. Department of Education directory information guidance](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/content/directory-information) | Public student names can be subject to directory-information notices and opt-outs. | Do not invent names, photos, roles, or approval; confirm permission before publishing a student's credit. |

## Editorial rules

- Each public credit should say **who** contributed, **what** they did, and the role or affiliation only if confirmed.
- Describe contributions through specific actions and who they serve. Do not use a special “featured” rank for the project's first contributor.
- Names, photos, and links are optional. No portrait is required to make a contribution feel substantial.
- An empty contribution area describes the work it can recognize; it does not imply a department or school official has endorsed this student-run project.
- In the Puck credits editor, select **Credits & Contributors**, open **Contribution Areas**, then open the area to edit its heading and description or use **People in This Area** to add and reorder names. Areas can be added, renamed, reordered, or removed without changing any person's group text.
- The introductory copy, jump link, recognition note, and correction card are also editable in the same Puck block. Changes save in the current browser and appear in the editor preview and on that browser's public page.
- Correct or remove a credit when a contributor requests it. The public “Suggest a credit” link goes to the Pioneer Clubs Support Center for review.

## External credit links

[MDN's `window.open()` guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/open) notes that browser settings and user preferences determine whether a link opens in a foreground or background tab or window. [MDN's focus documentation](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/focus) says a page cannot guarantee which window is frontmost. Browser-tab highlighting is exposed to extensions through the [Chrome Tabs API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/tabs/), not to ordinary sites. Trying to simulate a blinking tab or force focus back to Pioneer Clubs would therefore be unreliable. [W3C advises](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G201) warning users when a link opens a new tab; its [motion guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html) also identifies blinking as a distraction.

For Aarti's site and other external credits, use a normal link that requests a new tab, with a small visible external-link icon, an accessible “opens in a new tab” cue, and `rel="noopener noreferrer"`. The browser decides tab focus. The Pioneer Clubs page stays intact so visitors can return to it. Do not launch a popup or blink the page.

The visual concept uses the site's atom mark as a “many orbits, one community” motif. Matching contribution-area cards establish the project's student beginnings and make room for the network around it without placeholder people, a special rank, or false claims.
